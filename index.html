Background

About Me

I grew up in Rio Bravo, Tamaulipas — across the Rio Grande from Donna, TX, in the heart of the Rio Grande Valley. Growing up on the border meant growing up bilingual, bicultural, and fluent in navigating between worlds. That instinct for context — who the data affects, where people actually live — has shaped every research project I've touched since.

My career traces a deliberate arc from the border outward. I started in cross-border logistics at TRANCASA, then interned with the Economic Policy Institute in Silicon Valley, studying post-recession recovery for low-income families. At the Bush School I received the U.S. Congress–George H.W. Bush Fellowship and delivered a comparative security analysis to U.S. Southern Command. That researcher identity deepened at PERC (Texas A&M), where I built economic indices, co-authored a peer-reviewed study on Medicaid and recidivism, and directed a COVID-19 business impact survey. At Texas 2036 I translated complex data into legislative recommendations on workforce and criminal justice. Since January 2025 I've served as Data Scientist at the Texas Space Commission, engineering the state's first space workforce index.

I bring to big tech a combination that's genuinely rare: rigorous econometric training, production-grade data science skills (Python, SQL, Stata, ML), and a decade of translating ambiguous, high-stakes problems into decisions that moved policy. I work across sectors, communicate fluently to both technical and executive audiences, and carry a bilingual, international perspective built into everything I do. I'm looking for a team where measurement, causal thinking, and real-world impact are taken seriously.

Independent Research · New
More service jobs, less technical AI use — a subnational look
Carlos Ignacio Navarro · August 2026 · Read on this site →
Pairs Anthropic's Economic Index with OECD regional employment statistics across 279 regions in 35 countries. Within countries, regions with more service employment show a lower share of AI usage going to technical work — the reverse of the intuitive mapping from industry mix to technical AI use. Survives country fixed effects, 35 leave-one-country-out refits, a wild cluster bootstrap, an independent replication in Stata, and a second usage measure built from a different classification scheme.
Peer-Reviewed
The Effect of Public Health Insurance on Criminal Recidivism
Erkmen G. Aslim, Murat C. Mungan, Carlos I. Navarro, Han Yu · Journal of Policy Analysis and Management · 2021
Exploits variation in state Medicaid expansion decisions — and individual-level National Corrections Reporting Program data — to establish that access to public health insurance causally reduces recidivism among offenders convicted of violent and public-order crimes, driven by increased criminal-justice referrals to addiction treatment.
Book Chapter
The Value Chain: A Study of the Coffee Industry
William H. Byrnes, Nima Khodakarami, Carlos Navarro Perez · A Practical Guide to US Transfer Pricing, LexisNexis
A transfer pricing case study applying the Transactional Net Margin Method to a hypothetical specialty coffee roaster in Europe, tracing the full supply and value chain from cultivation to consumer and identifying comparable parties using Orbis financial data.
Policy Report
Opening Doors: The State of Higher Education in Texas Prisons
Luis Soberon & Carlos Ignacio Navarro · Texas 2036 · November 2024
Finds that participation in prison college programs reduces recidivism by up to 41.5% and yields a $1.61 return per $1 spent; recommends governance reforms, expanded community college access, and integrated data systems to scale higher education across TDCJ facilities.
Working Paper
Methodology for Constructing an Economic Index for the College Station-Bryan MSA
Dennis W. Jansen, Carlos I. Navarro, Andrew J. Rettenmaier, Tatevik Sekhposyan · Private Enterprise Research Center, Texas A&M University · October 2018
Presents the dynamic factor model methodology and data sources used to build a monthly coincident business-cycle index for the College Station-Bryan metropolitan area, using sales-tax collections as a proxy for retail activity when official monthly GDP data is unavailable.
Policy Report
Pandemic Misery Index: Economic and Health Implications of COVID-19
Erkmen G. Aslim & Carlos I. Navarro · Seidman Business Review, Vol. 27, Issue 1 · Winter 2021
Adapts the Pandemic Misery Index — combining unemployment rates with COVID-19 deaths per 10,000 — to Michigan MSAs, finding wide regional variation (PMI range: 11.82–26.09) and identifying policy effectiveness in managing simultaneous economic and public health burdens.
